# Comparison 6 — the cyclic port measured on the 38 cyclic targets

The gate [cyclic-port.md](../cyclic-port.md) left open. Three arms, same tier,
same seeds, same targets, same machine, run back to back:

- **base** — `master` at `414835e`, the pre-port tree
- **port** — `port/cyclic-remainder` at `c67b958`, the port with upstream's
  weight-1 closure harmonic
- **w5** — the same port at `6643614`, with only the CA-CA closure harmonic
  restored to this repo's weight of 5

The third arm was not planned: it tests the one hypothesis section 3 raised.

Sets B (18 backbone-cyclised) and C (20 disulfide-cyclised) from
`tests/validation/manifest.tsv`, smoke tier: 8 replicas x 200,000 steps,
`MaxRotamers=20`, seeds 1-8. Measured 2026-09-04, gcc 13.3.0 / Ubuntu 24.04 /
i7-12650H, 16 threads, two targets in parallel at 8 cores each.

## Headline: the ring holds, the speed mostly does not, the ranking slips

| | base | port | w5 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CA1-CAn median, set B | 3.82 A | 3.81 A | **3.82 A** | gate passes (crystal 3.83 A) |
| CA1-CAn per-target spread | flat | 3.62-4.04 | **flat** | w5 restores it |
| wall time, 38 targets | 2366 s | 1612 s | 1792 s | 1.47x / 1.32x, not 3-4x |
| avg fnc top5, set B | 0.438 | 0.382 | **0.431** | w5 recovers 87% |
| avg fnc top100, set B | 0.576 | 0.577 | **0.619** | w5 beats both |
| failed / hung replicas | 0/608 | 0/608 | 0/608 | unchanged |

**The weight was the cause.** Upstream's split of the closure restraint into
three weight-1 terms is what cost set B its mid-rank ranking; putting the CA-CA
harmonic back at weight 5 recovers it and improves on the pre-port tree at
top1 and top100.

## 1. Macrocycle geometry — the gate

This is why `cyclic_energy` was repaired rather than ported verbatim. The
shipped ADFRsuite binary closes the ring at a median 2.52 A; the question was
whether taking upstream's structure would drag us there.

| source | median CA1-CAn | n |
|---|---|---|
| crystallographic ligands | 3.83 A | 18 |
| base (`master`) | 3.82 A | 144 poses |
| **port** (weight 1) | **3.81 A** | 144 poses |
| **w5** (weight 5) | **3.82 A** | 144 poses |
| ADFRsuite 1.0 (measured in cyclic-port.md) | 2.52 A | 432 poses |

No collapse. The repair holds in the poses, not only in
`tests/cyclic_closure_test.cpp`.

The distributions are not the same shape, though. `base` sits almost exactly on
3.82 A for every target; `port` spreads from 3.62 to 4.04:

| target | crystal | port | base | target | crystal | port | base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1sfi | 3.81 | 3.70 | 3.82 | 3avk | 3.79 | 3.71 | 3.85 |
| 3av9 | 3.90 | 3.78 | 3.83 | 3avm | 3.83 | 3.78 | 3.82 |
| 3ava | 3.88 | 3.90 | 3.82 | 3avn | 3.82 | 3.75 | 3.88 |
| 3avb | 3.91 | 3.73 | 3.82 | 3p8f | 4.28 | 3.82 | 3.84 |
| 3avf | 3.82 | 3.97 | 3.82 | 3wne | 3.78 | 3.84 | 4.19 |
| 3avg | 3.86 | 3.94 | 3.89 | 3zgc | 3.82 | 3.79 | 3.82 |
| 3avh | 3.84 | 3.87 | 3.82 | 4k1e | 3.80 | 3.82 | 3.82 |
| 3avi | 3.86 | 3.77 | 3.82 | 4kel | 3.80 | 4.04 | 3.82 |
| 3avj | 3.89 | 3.62 | 3.88 | 5xn3 | 3.75 | 3.82 | 3.83 |

That is the expected consequence of the weight change: `base` concentrates the
restraint in one harmonic at weight 5, `port` spreads it over three terms at
weight 1 each. Same centre, looser spring. The `w5` arm puts the spring back
and the spread collapses to ~0.00 per target, matching `base`.

## 2. Speed — 1.47x, not 3-4x

| set | base | port | w5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| B (backbone) | 1140 s | 849 s | 940 s |
| C (disulfide) | 1226 s | 763 s | 852 s |
| all 38 | 2366 s | **1612 s (1.47x)** | **1792 s (1.32x)** |

Set C calibrates the noise floor. Its targets do not cyclise the backbone, so
`port` and `w5` run identical code there -- and they differ by 763 s vs 852 s,
**12%**. Most of the port-vs-w5 gap is machine noise, not the weight. The
base-vs-port gap of 47% is well outside it.

cyclic-port.md measured upstream's binary at 3-4x on cyclic targets and flagged
the risk that the gap came from the broken branch short-circuiting the
restraint. It did, mostly. Repairing the arithmetic returns the geometry and
gives back most of the speed; what remains comes from the disulfide rework and
the rotamer/grid changes rather than from the closure term.

Per-target times carry contention noise -- two targets share 16 cores, and
individual ratios run from 0.50x (3avh) to 2.62x (6bae). Only the totals are
worth quoting, and only to two significant figures.

## 3. Docking quality — set B loses mid-rank fnc

Average fnc against the reference scorer, matched targets only:

| set | rank | base | port | w5 | w5 − base | w5 − port |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B | top1 | 0.268 | 0.266 | **0.283** | +0.015 | +0.017 |
| B | top5 | 0.438 | 0.382 | 0.431 | −0.007 | **+0.049** |
| B | top10 | 0.490 | 0.406 | 0.470 | −0.020 | **+0.065** |
| B | top100 | 0.576 | 0.577 | **0.619** | +0.043 | +0.042 |
| C | top1 | 0.246 | 0.246 | 0.246 | −0.000 | +0.000 |
| C | top5 | 0.325 | 0.307 | 0.307 | −0.019 | +0.000 |
| C | top10 | 0.345 | 0.361 | 0.361 | +0.015 | +0.000 |
| C | top100 | 0.437 | 0.430 | 0.430 | −0.007 | +0.000 |

Two things to read off it.

**Set C is the control, and it is exact.** Those targets do not cyclise the
backbone, so `cyclic_energy` is never called and `port` and `w5` run identical
code. Every set C figure agrees to three decimals. The weight change is
isolated to what it claims to touch, which is what makes the set B column
interpretable at all.

**On set B, weight 5 recovers the loss.** `port` gave up 0.056 at top5 and
0.084 at top10 against the pre-port tree; `w5` gives back 0.049 and 0.065 of
that -- 87% and 76% -- and lands *above* the pre-port tree at top1 (+0.015) and
top100 (+0.043). The hypothesis section 3 raised in the two-arm version of this
document is confirmed: the split into three weight-1 terms was the cause.

The threshold counts disagree with the averages, and both belong in the record:

| fnc >= 0.5 | base | port | w5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| top1 | 4/38 | 2/38 | 3/38 |
| top5 | 10/38 | 3/38 | 5/38 |
| top10 | 14/38 | 9/38 | 10/38 |
| top100 | 22/38 | 23/38 | 23/38 |

`w5` lifts the average on set B but still puts fewer targets over the 0.5 line
than `base` at top5 and top10. The distribution changed shape, not just its
centre: many targets improve a little, fewer cross the threshold.

**This tier cannot support a stronger claim than the direction.** run_set.sh's
own header says the smoke tier "says nothing about docking quality", and the
per-target swings dwarf the aggregates -- 3wne −0.43 and 5wxr −0.41 against
5eoc +0.33 and 4kel +0.32, on 8 replicas each. What the third arm establishes
is which knob moves set B, not by how much.

## 4. What did not move

- 0 failed and 0 hung replicas across all 608 runs in both arms.
- Fold fixture: `port` reproduces upstream `cyclic` and the shipped ADFRsuite
  binary bit for bit (ATOM md5 `abf915bc…`, energy 9.274271), where `base`
  gives `6a438d0a…` / 5.936278.
- 3Q47 redock: `port` reproduces `cyclic`'s exact pose and energy (run 1,
  −29.1304), RMSD 0.88 A against `base`'s 0.70 A.

## Conclusion

1. **The gate passes.** Repairing `cyclic_energy` rather than porting it
   verbatim keeps the macrocycle at the crystallographic distance. The decision
   recorded in cyclic-port.md was the right one.
2. **Most of upstream's speed was the defect.** 1.47x survives the repair, not
   3-4x. Anyone quoting the shipped binary's cyclic throughput is quoting a
   collapsed ring.
3. **The closure weight was the cause, and it is fixed.** Upstream's split into
   three weight-1 terms cost set B its mid-rank ranking; restoring the CA-CA
   harmonic to weight 5 recovers 87% of the top5 loss and 76% of the top10
   loss, and beats the pre-port tree at top1 and top100. Landed as `6643614`.
   Set C, running identical code in both arms, agrees to three decimals --
   the change is isolated.
4. **Nothing regressed in robustness.** No new crashes, no new hangs, across
   all 1,824 runs in three arms.
5. **What is still open**: the threshold counts (`fnc >= 0.5`) still favour the
   pre-port tree at top5 and top10 even where the averages do not, and the
   smoke tier cannot settle that. It needs the `reduced` tier at minimum.

## Protocol

```sh
# targets (needs ADFRsuite + network); 38/38 prepared, 0 failed
tests/validation/prepare_all.sh ~/adcp-bench/targets BC

# arms
git worktree add ~/adcp-bench/base master
cmake -S ~/adcp-bench/base -B ~/adcp-bench/base/build -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release
cmake --build ~/adcp-bench/base/build -j8

for arm in port base w5; do
  tests/validation/run_set.sh <bin> ~/adcp-bench/targets ~/adcp-bench/results-$arm smoke BC
done

# measurements
tests/validation/ring_geometry.py ~/adcp-bench/results-port \
    --vs ~/adcp-bench/results-base <18 set-B ids>
tests/validation/score_all.sh ~/adcp-bench/results-port    # and -base
tests/validation/report.py ~/adcp-bench/results-port --tsv port.tsv
```

Timings come from each target's `run_info.txt` `seconds` field. The arms ran
back to back on an otherwise idle machine, in the order `port`, `base`, `w5`.
